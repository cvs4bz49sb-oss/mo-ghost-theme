The Puritans

Perkins to the Mathers: the godly preachers of England, Scotland and New England (1570–1720)

This shelf holds the ministers who wanted the Church of England reformed further and the Nonconformists and New Englanders who came after them. They wrote sermons and treatises of conscience, catechisms, commentaries and controversy. Most are read here as they were first printed.

1 · The press

A shelf of printed sermons

Every dated edition on the shelf stands in the column of its year. Its color shows where it was printed. The Puritans were preachers who printed. A sermon heard on Sunday was often a book by the next term.

    2 · Where they printed and preached

    Three countries

    London printed most of the shelf. The godly also printed at the universities and in Scotland. Exiles printed in the Low Countries. Some of their ministers had gone to New England and printed there.

      3 · The brotherhood

      A chain of preachers

      Perkins drew the order of salvation as A Golden Chain. His hearers made a chain of another kind. By the accounts of the time, each of these five was brought to faith by the preaching of the one before. By his own account, a sixth was brought to faith by a book.
