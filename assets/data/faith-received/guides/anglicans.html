From the Reformers to Pusey (1485–1882)

The divines of the Church of England: the Reformers who wrote its Prayer Book and Articles, the bishops who defended it against Rome and Geneva, the Caroline preachers, the Restoration churchmen, the Nonjurors who would not swear to a new king, and the Oxford Movement.

1 · The formularies

Six books on the lectern

The Church of England has no single confession. It has its formularies: a Prayer Book, Articles, Homilies, an Ordinal, a Catechism, and Jewel’s Apology for them all. Most of what is on this shelf expounds, defends or argues over one of them.

2 · In print

A church that argued in print

Each mark is a work on this shelf. It stands at the year its copy was printed. The tall years are the crises: the bishops impeached in 1641 and the Revolution of 1688.

    4 · The Christian year

    Andrewes preaches the year

    Lancelot Andrewes preached at court on the feasts of the Prayer Book for thirty-six years. After his death Laud and Buckeridge printed ninety-six of the sermons in the order of the year. Each bead is one of them at the date it was preached. The oldest lie at the center.
