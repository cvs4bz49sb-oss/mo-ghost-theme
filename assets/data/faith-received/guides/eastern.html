The Eastern Fathers

Patrologia Orientalis · the churches of the East in their own languages

René Graffin and François Nau began the Patrologia Orientalis at Paris in 1904. It prints the writings of the Eastern churches in the languages they have come down in. These are Syriac, Arabic, Coptic, Ge’ez, Armenian, Georgian, Greek and Slavonic. It holds homilies and hymns, lives of the saints, chronicles and liturgies. They run from Irenaeus in the second century to Paul of Aleppo in the seventeenth. Here the original is read beside English.

1 · The series

Fifty-seven tomes

René Graffin, a priest of Paris, began the Patrologia Syriaca in 1894. In 1904 he and François Nau opened the Patrologia Orientalis to every Eastern church. Each text is printed with a translation facing it. Most translations are French. Others are Latin, English or Italian. The series is still being published. The shelf holds the three volumes of the Syriaca and the tomes from 1 to 59, less 54 and 55.

2 · The languages

Eight languages

Each text is printed in the language it has come down in. That is not always the one it was written in. Irenaeus and Hippolytus wrote Greek and are read here in Armenian and Georgian. Beside each language stands the opening of one of its works with an English translation.

      4 · One preacher

      Severus at Antioch

      As patriarch of Antioch from 512 to 518 Severus preached the 125 sermons known as his Cathedral Homilies. They are kept in the Syriac translation of Jacob of Edessa. The series printed them a few at a time and out of their order.

        5 · The year of the saints

        Three books of the saints

        A synaxarium gives the saints to be remembered on each day of the year. Its year has twelve months of thirty days and five days more. The series printed three of them a volume or two at a time. They are the Copts’ in Arabic, the Ethiopians’ in Ge’ez and the Armenians’ that Ter Israel compiled.
