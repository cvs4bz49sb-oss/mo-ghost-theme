The Westminster Assembly

The divines at Westminster (1643–1652)

In 1643 Parliament called divines to Westminster to advise it on the worship, government and doctrine of the Church of England. Over nine years they wrote the Directory for Public Worship, a Form of Church Government, the Confession of Faith and two Catechisms. Here are the Assembly’s own texts, its minutes, and the books its members wrote.

1 · The sittings

Nine years at Westminster

The minutes record each day the Assembly met. Each mark is one of those days. It met almost every weekday from 1643 to 1649. From then to March 1652 it met on Thursdays only as a committee to examine ministers. The gilt marks are the days its work was done.

    2 · Where they came from

    From the parishes and the colleges

    The divines served in parishes and colleges, cathedrals and congregations. London comes first. Cambridge and Oxford follow. Then come the shires. Last come the Scots: a professor at Glasgow, another at St Andrews, and ministers of Fife and Edinburgh.

    3 · What they wrote

    While they sat, they printed

    Every book of theirs on the shelf stands at the year it was printed. The press ran hardest while the Assembly sat. It printed sermons preached before Parliament on its fast days, books on the government of the church, and the practical divinity of the English pulpit. Choose a kind to list its books.

    4 · The Confession

    Thirty-three chapters

    The Confession of Faith was presented to Parliament in December 1646. Its thirty-three chapters run from Holy Scripture to the Last Judgment. Each column is a chapter. Each stone is one of its sections. Choose a chapter to read it and to see the divines’ own books on the same subject.

    5 · The debates

    Who spoke

    The scribes set a speaker’s name before each speech they took down. The minutes do this mostly in 1643 and 1644. Each bar is a member. Its length counts the speeches the minutes give him. Its color is his party.

    6 · The Annotations

    Notes on every chapter

    London stationers asked a committee of the Commons for leave to print notes fitted to the King James Bible. The chair of the Committee for Religion wrote to some divines and invited others in person. Several of them sat in the Assembly. They divided the books among themselves. Their Annotations upon All the Books of the Old and New Testament came out in 1645.

    Methodology

    Each square is a chapter. The darker the square, the longer its notes.
