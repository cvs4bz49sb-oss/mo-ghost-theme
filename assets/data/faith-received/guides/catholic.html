1 · The schools

The schools after Trent

After the Council of Trent Catholic theology was taught in schools: the Thomists of the Dominicans and the Discalced Carmelites, the Scotists of the Franciscan houses, the Jesuits after Suárez and Molina, the Augustinians of Louvain and the Sorbonne. Each stream is a school. Its width is the number of its writers on this shelf at work in each decade.

2 · Where they taught

From Salamanca to the Roman College

The schools lived in universities and colleges. Suárez taught at Rome, Alcalá, Salamanca and Coimbra. Bellarmine taught at Louvain before Rome. Jansen came from Louvain to his see at Ypres.

    3 · The council

    The turn at Trent

    The Council of Trent met in twenty-five sessions over eighteen years. It met in three periods under three popes. Its decrees on Scripture, justification and the sacraments are the ground of everything on this shelf after 1563.

      4 · The Thomists

      The Summa, treatise by treatise

      From Cajetan onward the Dominican masters taught by commenting on the Summa of Thomas question by question. Each row is a commentary on this shelf. Each column is one of the Summa’s treatises in Thomas’s order. The darker the square, the more pages the commentator gave it.

      5 · The Scotists

      Scotus against Thomas

      The Franciscan schools read the Sentences through Duns Scotus and defined themselves against the Thomists. Juan de Rada’s Controversies between St Thomas and Scotus (Cologne, 1620) sets the two doctors against each other on the four books of the Sentences.

        6 · The Jesuits

        Cornelius a Lapide’s Bible

        Cornelius a Lapide taught Scripture at Louvain and then at the Roman College. His commentary on the Bible fills twenty-one volumes on this shelf. Each square is a book of the Bible. The column inside it rises with the pages he gave that book volume by volume.

        7 · The method

        How a disputation is built

        Francisco Suárez published his Metaphysical Disputations at Salamanca in 1597. It was the first complete metaphysics written as a system rather than as a commentary on Aristotle. It was read in Catholic and Protestant schools alike.

          8 · The quarrel over grace

          Grace and free will

          How does grace move a free will without forcing it? The Dominicans held that grace is efficacious of itself. The Jesuits after Molina held that it is efficacious through the consent God foresees. Rome examined the question from 1597 to 1607 and let both schools teach. Jansen’s Augustinus (Louvain, 1640) opened a second quarrel, which ran to Unigenitus in 1713.

          9 · Merit

          Why good works merit

          After Trent the Catholic schools agreed that the good works of the justified truly merit eternal life. They disagreed about why. Merit de condigno is owed in justice. Merit de congruo is rewarded because it is fitting. Is a work done in grace worthy because God accepts it and promises the reward? Or does grace make the work itself worthy?
